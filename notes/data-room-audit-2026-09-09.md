# Audit of the existing data room

Audited the prose in `dataroom-live.json`, generated at 2026-09-09T20:56:14.075Z, against its evidence, the content module, the feed services and the renderer. This is a snapshot audit, not a new production observation. No tests were run and no backend or renderer fixes were made. Numbers below are audit evidence, not new page prose.

## Overview

- "The platform runs a floor on itself, one market prices its weekly pulse, and everything that market settles against is published here, live, from the same database that serves the site." The singular framing is obsolete: `rates.metrics` contains Active traders, Telarchy revenue (USD), Profitable forecasters and Outside owners deciding; `calendar.dates` contains daily, weekly and monthly settlements. `buildBaseRates()` reads all defined self-workspace metrics with recent logs. The feed and `platformStats()` are cached, separately, for 60 seconds. Say readings and use the publication timestamp, not an implied fresh measurement on every request.
- "Nothing on this page is a projection and nothing here argues a position." The lapse dates are conditional calculations assuming no new trades; marked profit depends on current market values. The old mechanism and Plans sections also explicitly argue a position. Distinguish observations, marks and assumptions rather than promising that the whole page has no projections.
- "The numbers are small." This is a time-sensitive assessment rather than a rule; the evidence currently includes 9 active verified traders. Keep the promise to publish small numbers without fixing today's assessment into prose.
- "Publishing them anyway is the point: the floor's charter promises that a week near zero means nobody showed up, and a data room that only published flattering figures would make that promise a lie." Near zero means few qualifying paid-record traders crossed the volume threshold, not that nobody visited or traded. The snapshot has 2,576 filtered requests in the last week and 246 platform trade rows. Keep the publication promise and remove the false equivalence and its defence.
- The opening explanation of owner metrics and proposal pricing is consistent with the intended product in `docs/vision.md`, but it repeats the following section. It is reduced to a factual description covering company and personal use, with participant symmetry stated once.

## What it is for

- "As AI takes over more of the operational work, the scarce thing is not the ability to act." and "It is knowing which actions are worth taking." These are a thesis, not observations in this feed. Remove.
- "You cannot verify a proposer's judgment, because an AI has no skin in the game and a person pitching their own project has the wrong kind, so the answer is not to trust the proposal." Unsupported categorical claims about motivations and verification. The shared participant model does not establish either. Remove.
- "It is to price it, in front of forecasters who lose money when they are wrong." Misstates credits as money at risk. `docs/legal/season-0-rules.md`, "Free means free", says credits are play money, cannot be bought, have no cash value and cannot be redeemed. Prizes are separate. Preserve those operative distinctions.
- "That is the whole mechanism." The following conditional-market explanation is a useful mechanism summary but not a full account of access, settlement or prize rules. The owner/participant/priced-impact sentences, futarchy comparison and name derivation belong in product documentation, not this evidence page.
- "Choosing the right metric is still the owner's job, and the system will faithfully optimise whatever metric it is given, so Goodhart is a live failure mode rather than a solved one." The responsibility and risk are worth retaining. Faithful optimisation is not established by activity data or by having markets. Remove that implied performance guarantee.
- "Whether a model is internally deceptive is not something a market on outcomes can see." Retain the limitation, without claiming a solution elsewhere.
- "The slice this takes is the control surface: every proposed action priced against stated goals before it is taken, and the record of who was right kept in public." The system does not enforce that all real-world actions wait for pricing. Private workspaces exist; records are not universally public. `platform-stats.ts` queries all workspaces, while `trading-by-day.ts` explicitly restricts public ones.
- "An owner buys a calibrated number before committing." No calibration measurement is published. A price is not proof of calibration.
- "A forecaster buys a benchmark where being right pays in money rather than points, and where their track record is public and portable." Credits and prize eligibility are conflated; not every profitable participant receives a prize. Public portability is not evidenced by these blocks. Remove the sales claim and retain the prize-rule route.
- "The trader side is being built first, because with no users at all the scarce resource is a stranger's first minute, and the only first minute on offer is trading a real company's roadmap." The snapshot has 61 auth accounts and 256 participant records. Those do not prove external customers, but they do not support "no users at all" either. This is an outdated strategy argument.

## The readings

- "Every number this floor prices, and everything it has done." `buildBaseRates()` limits logs to its recent eight-week range and omits metrics with no reading. It does not publish all history or every action.
- "Each is drawn from the hourly reading the platform takes of itself, one point per day, with the dated things the owner did marked on the day they happened." `self-sync.ts` records hourly observations; `base-rates.ts` selects the last logged value per UTC day. The current day is incomplete. Events include approvals, declines and announcements, not just completed work. `floor-events.ts` limits the final list to 12 recent events, with deliveries drawn from a bounded decision query.
- "The eight weekly readings are printed under each line, because a shape is not a base rate and 'how far does this normally move in a week' is a number." The readings exist, but they are levels, not weekly changes. `buildBaseRates()` constructs rolling intervals ending at request time, not fixed calendar weeks or market settle dates. The renderer prints only the first and last dates underneath. Retain the definition of the samples, remove the argument and recommend individual dates.
- "A week the sync did not run is a hole rather than a repeat of the week before, because a week nobody measured and a week nothing happened are different facts." The weekly array correctly uses null. The daily chart connects gaps up to three days and breaks longer gaps. Missing readings do not prove a sync outage: the metric may not yet have existed. Preserve missing-versus-zero without diagnosing the cause.

## What moved it

- "Announcements and decisions around the readings. These dates provide context; they do not establish what caused a change." Correct qualification. Retain it beside the readings. Retitle the dated list to avoid the causal implication of "What moved it". The snapshot's 12 entries run from 2 to 9 September; this is not a complete event history.

## What is scheduled

- "What has already been committed, between now and the settle dates on the board: every open book with the day it settles, every proposal on the ballot with the day it must be decided by." `buildCalendar()` includes only active, unresolved, non-voided baseline books in the self workspace, deduplicates metric/target pairs, excludes undated proposals and removes past dates. Pending proposals are not commitments to do the work. Deadlines are not guaranteed decision or delivery events.
- "Nothing here is a plan typed in for this page; each row is a date something in the database falls due on." True of `dates`, not the whole block: it also renders outreach stages. The snapshot contains 30 stages (9 sent, 21 ready). These are stored statuses, not dated outcomes or verified customer adoption. Name both parts.

## The window

- "Every number this floor prices counts a trailing window, so part of the next reading is already fixed by rows that exist today." Profit includes qualifying open positions whose marked values can change. Existing rows do not fix that contribution. Revenue and trading also use different windows, while the outside-owner count is distinct workspaces, not decision rows.
- "Those rows are here, one at a time: what each verified participant has traded this week, the day each counted trader falls out of their own week if they never trade again, what every participant is up or down, every undecided proposal on an outside floor with the day it decides by, and every payment on the revenue rail." Several limits are missing:
  - `tradersBlock()` uses paid `manifold_link` claims, includes zero spend and sums absolute cost over all trade kinds. It tests lapse at daily offsets from computation time, then discards the time. These are sampled dates, not exact expiry dates; a threshold crossing can occur earlier than the returned day.
  - `forecastersBlock()` lists only entries returned by the board calculation and removes house accounts; it does not enumerate every participant. The snapshot has 28 profit entries versus 256 participant records.
  - `platformMarkedProfit()` uses resolutions in the trailing 30 days plus open positions that satisfy the board's settlement-horizon filter. Its end is 366 days ahead, not unbounded "every open market". House means platform-admin or platform-operated here.
  - `ownersBlock()` covers public outside floors only and emits a row per proposal. `outsideOwnersDeciding7d()` counts distinct workspaces, includes private ones, requires the owner to decide, and excludes starter proposals. The pending list neither uses the starter exclusion nor provides the existing decisions' expiry dates. The snapshot's wallpaper-animator proposal cannot by itself establish future qualifying adoption.
  - `revenueBlock()` covers 30 days and excludes platform-admin buyers, not all platform-operated buyers. It includes incomplete purchases; `revenue30dUsd` counts completed purchases only, dated by completion with creation as fallback. The snapshot's sole published payment is a completed $0 row on 30 August, not a paying customer.
- "Nobody is named. A participant is an entry in a list of numbers, a payment is an amount and a date, and a floor appears under the name its own page already carries." Structural identity fields are appropriately absent, and the workspace field is a public slug. However, payment status is also carried, and proposal titles and event text are unrestricted human text. There is no general name-redaction pass. Do not claim the code guarantees names can never occur in free text.
- The thresholds are carried (100 credits each), but duration and inclusion metadata are not. The numeric-prose prohibition prevents repairing missing machine-derived definitions by typing durations into the page. Add those fields and captions as proposed below; the rewrite gives the qualitative distinctions without inventing fields.

## Who is here

- "The floor this platform runs on itself measures weekly active verified traders: distinct participants with a public Manifold profile synced to their account who have traded at least a hundred credits anywhere on the platform in the trailing seven days." Wrong verified set. `paidManifoldLinkAgents()` reads paid claims in `earn_claims`, not links in `record_links`. Linking an unqualified profile has been possible since September. The amount and window match `WEEKLY_TRADER_MIN_CREDITS` and `computePlatformStats()`, but should remain machine-owned.
- "Verified means each counted trader maps to a public profile anyone can inspect on the leaderboard." The metric checks the paid claim, not current public-profile accessibility or the existence of a badge. `recordProviders.ts` gates payment on account age, bot flag and activity; a free link is not payment. State the actual eligibility test.
- "The hundred-credit floor keeps a one-credit gesture from counting, because signup credits are free." The threshold is real; the defence is unnecessary, and repeated volume can count without establishing independent demand. Keep the threshold in the block.
- "The number syncs once a day from the same database that serves this page, and the owner cannot edit it." The stats endpoint computes on cache refresh; historical readings are hourly self-sync. `self-sync.ts` replaced the old daily job. This is not an immutable-source guarantee against the operator changing code, records or configuration.
- "Four numbers stand between a stranger and that definition, and each is a filter on the one above it." Not a cohort. `funnel()` divides independently obtained aggregates. Auth accounts are `authUser` rows; verified participants are earn claims over participant identities, which can register by API. Even the verified/account step is not a proved subset.
- "Page loads count what the visit rollup holds, which starts later than the accounts do, so the first step is not a cohort and the percentage under it is arithmetic between two published figures rather than a claim that those accounts came out of those loads." The caveat about dates is warranted, but does not repair the remaining identity mismatch or turn filtered requests into page loads. Drop the funnel rather than leave misleading conversions.

## Traction

- "What has happened so far, in full." The block mixes all-time table counts with only 60 days of signups. `signupsByDay` sums to 47 in the snapshot, not the 61 total accounts. `DataRoomPage.tsx` plots the truncated cumulative sum as "Accounts, running total". Its caption does not supply the missing starting balance.
- "Participants include automated ones, which are most of them: the platform's own trading agents hold accounts like anyone else, and a count that hid them would be a nicer number and a false one." Inclusion is correct, but `traction()` never classifies automation or platform ownership. The difference between 256 participant rows and 61 auth accounts does not prove a bot majority. Remove the unsupported composition claim.
- "Proposals are the jobs side of the floor. Anyone may propose a piece of work and a price, the markets price what approving it would do to the metric, and the owner approves or declines with a written reason. Every decision is public and sits on the floor next to the market that priced it." Repeated verbatim in Proposals. Access is permission-gated. `approveProposal()` does not require a written reason; `declineProposal()` requires one only for a workspace with a charter. Private decisions are not public. Replace both copies with the scope of the actual totals.

## Trading

- "How busy the place actually is, day by day: the trades placed, the credits they moved, and how many people placed them." `buildTradingByDay()` counts participant IDs, not people, and only currently public workspaces over 120 days. It excludes `redeem`. Activity is not cash revenue or external demand.
- "The trader count is a threshold on this; the shape underneath it is what says whether the count is about to move." False equivalence: the active-trader metric includes every workspace and trade kind and filters paid Manifold claims. The daily charts apply different filters. The charts can provide context, not directly reconstruct that count.

## Traffic

- "Every document load the site serves is logged by the site itself." `shouldLogVisit()` excludes `/admin`; the catch-all log can also contain missing asset requests. `humanVisitFilter()` does not call `isPageLoad()`. These are filtered requests, not a complete count of document loads.
- "There is no third-party analytics on Telarchy, no tracking cookie and no advertising network, which the privacy policy states and this is the same log that backs it." The local visitor-log implementation does not prove a site-wide absence of other tracking. This audit does not certify that negative. Keep the scope and privacy of the published aggregate, not a broad infrastructure claim.
- "Crawlers and vulnerability scanners are the majority of raw hits before a launch, so both this page and the owner's own cockpit drop anything whose user-agent looks like a bot and any scanner probe path." The supplied feed contains no raw-hit denominator to substantiate "majority". The filter is a regex heuristic, not detection of every bot or probe.
- "They call the same filter, so the public number cannot flatter and the private one cannot differ." Shared filtering is correct; numerical identity is not guaranteed. Public retained daily totals use `greatest(old, new)` on rollup, live windows query raw rows, and caches differ. A filter can still overcount people.
- "Individual visit rows are deleted after thirty days, per the privacy policy." `maintenance.ts` implements a 30-day cutoff in `runDailyMaintenance()`, scheduled daily according to its service contract. This supports the retention policy, not deletion at each row's exact expiry instant or proof that the job ran in production. The rewrite states the aggregate's fields without copying a numeric retention parameter into prose.
- "What survives is a daily count of visits and distinct addresses, kept forever: no address, no country, no path, no referrer." Correct shape for `trafficDaily`; "forever" is stronger than demonstrating no expiry in this rollup. Remove the indefinite operational guarantee.
- "History therefore starts on the day the rollup shipped rather than at the beginning of time." Incorrect inference. Rollup reads all retained raw rows, so it can backfill dates before deployment. `keptSince` is the earliest stored day, currently 11 August.
- "Which channel a visitor came from is deliberately not published. It names outreach that has not happened yet, and the same is true of who signed up, so signup counts are here and the people behind them are not." Identities and channels are absent. The claim that referrers name future outreach is unsupported. Retain the boundary, remove its rationale.

## Proposals

The duplicated mechanism paragraph has the problems noted under Traction. `contracts()` queries all non-removed proposals without a visibility or ownership filter. It includes `declined_spam` in declined. The snapshot reports 41 proposals, 25 approved, 4 declined, 12 pending and $1,530 approved asks. None of these prove independent customers, completed work or payments. The renderer's "Declined, with a written reason" exceeds the conditional guarantee in `declineProposal()` and needs a separate change.

## Shipping

- "The change log is the git history of the repository this site is built from, regenerated on every deploy." It is generated by `scripts/build-changelog.mjs`, not from live tables. Generation date is not evidence that each commit deployed on its author date.
- "Nothing here is curated: the bars are every commit, and the entries are the commit subjects as they were written." The generator uses `git log --no-merges`, author dates, a `[private]` filter and a 150-entry quoted cap. The supplied feed has 450 counted changes but only 12 quoted subjects; it is not a complete textual history. The local generator's normal cap does not explain that short supplied list; do not invent an explanation.
- "A commit whose message says it is private is counted in the pace and never quoted, which exists so that a change that cannot be named does not have to be described vaguely instead." Only the literal `[private]` marker, case-insensitive in subject or body, is recognised. The rationale is not useful to either reader.
- "Everything else is published the moment it deploys." Older named entries can fall beyond the quoted cap. Commit count also does not measure customer value or staffing. Drop the block from the page, retaining its public feed route.

## Plans

- "The first season starts on 22 August 2026 and ends on 1 October 2026." The dates agree with `docs/seasons.md`, but are typed into prose and not carried by any season block here. The start is already past at the snapshot. Link to `/season` for current dates.
- "It pays real money to the traders who are most accurate over that window, scored on settled profit, meaning what the markets that actually resolved during the season paid you minus what you paid on them, rather than on volume or on marks that have not settled." Settled profit, not marks, is the broad scoring rule. It is not a calibration measure and eligibility, positive score, minimum payout, refunds and own-pool adjustments matter. Link the full rules instead of treating this as a sufficient payment promise.
- "Liquidity ramps up over the first three weeks rather than opening deep." A historical policy stated as current future work. The snapshot has no liquidity-ramp execution history; the period has elapsed.
- "A contest is the cheapest way to find out whether people will forecast for real stakes." Neither comparative cost nor real stakes is supported; entry uses free play credits.
- "The rules a contestant reads are published, and Season 0 says in its own text that we may change them while it runs: five amendments have landed so far, each announced on the season page before it took effect and each written to increase what is paid rather than to reduce anyone's standing." The fixed count is stale and forbidden prose data. The 1 September cutoff-removal amendment in `docs/legal/season-0-rules.md` explicitly says it can reduce scores and moved three scores, by up to 12.90 credits and about $10 of prize. Announcement timing is an obligation, not proved by the feed's recent event list. Preserve experimental status and the announcement obligation, not the false no-harm history.
- "Until the trader side pulls, the owner side stays deliberately quiet." An unsupported strategic narrative. The snapshot records founder outreach and public outside-floor proposals.
- "Creating a workspace is self-serve, capped at three floors per account, and a new floor is born unlisted: live, tradeable and shareable by link, but on the front page only once a human puts it there." `docs/vision.md` says unlisted floors cannot trade, even for their owners, until published. The cap excludes platform admins and explicit visibility at creation is honoured. Publication is available to API identities too. Remove onboarding rules from this page rather than publish stale ones.
- "So a floor arrives as a conversation rather than as a signup." and "The order is intentional: a two-sided market is bootstrapped one side at a time, and a floor with no traders is worth nothing to the company standing on it." Arguments about acquisition and value, not evidence.
- "The nearer work is the floor itself." and the sentence beginning "A visitor should be able to arrive knowing nothing" are aspirations with no committed date in the feed. The latter also promises an answer and a bet within a minute without evidence.
- "Every question asked of a floor is kept with the answer it got, because a question is a gap in the page said in a visitor's own words, and that is the highest-signal data a pre-launch product makes." No question/answer block or completeness evidence is published here; the signal ranking is opinion. Remove.
- "After that, the second company." and "One live floor proves the mechanism works and proves nothing about whether it transfers, and the honest version of this page in three months either names more companies or explains why it does not." There are 4 public floors in the snapshot, but no customer/company ownership breakdown. A live floor proves neither effectiveness nor transfer. The relative-date promise has no starting date or evidence field.

## Risks

- "There is no revenue." The latest revenue reading is $0 for a trailing window, not an all-time company statement. Keep the observed value in `rates`.
- "The platform charges nothing today, the business model is unsettled, and the season's prize pool is an operator cost rather than an investment anyone has made." Paid liquidity exists (`platform-stats.ts`, `liquidityPurchases`, `docs/liquidity-purchases.md`). The feed lacks costs, investment and business-model status. Keep the distinction between revenue, volume and prize payments; state which accounts are missing.
- "There is one company on the floor besides Telarchy itself, and it is run by the same person who runs Telarchy." The 4 public-floor count and outside pending slug make the old census unsafe. Neither proves how many independent companies exist. No ownership census is supplied.
- "That is the fastest way to get a real floor and also the weakest possible evidence that anyone else wants one." Unsupported superlatives. The important limit is that house activity does not establish outside demand.
- "The metric this platform prices itself on is gameable in principle, which is why it counts verified profiles and a credit floor rather than raw signups, and why the resolution route is public." There are several metrics and verification means a paid claim. Keep gaming risk and the public route, remove the mechanism defence.
- "It remains a proxy for something less measurable, and Goodhart applies to the operator as much as to anyone." Keep the metric-design limitation in plain words.
- "The mechanism is prediction markets, which carries a regulatory question in some jurisdictions when real money is involved. Season credits are not securities and the season is a skill contest with published rules, but this is a live legal question rather than a settled one." This audit cannot establish legal classification. Preserve the unresolved-status warning and the operative play-money/prize distinction. Do not offer the categorical securities conclusion as a finding from platform data.
- "Everything here is built by one person." The feed has no staffing field and the generated log removes author identities. It does not establish the claim.
- "The shipping pace on this page is evidence of speed and equally evidence of a single point of failure." Commit frequency proves neither delivery speed nor sole-person dependency. Staffing and continuity remain unpublished, not certified facts.

## Checking these numbers

- "This whole page, prose and figures together, is one public read at `/api/data-room`. No account, no key, no cookie." The public route is supported by `route-policy.ts`; preserve it. Authentication is not required; this does not claim a browser never sends a cookie.
- "If a number here does not match that response, the response is right." The response is the renderer's source, not an infallible measurement. Snapshot arithmetic and scope limitations above show why agreement alone is not correctness.
- The statistics, floor-payload and help routes are valid checking routes. Retain all of them. Replace "An agent should read those routes rather than scrape this page" with a direct instruction applicable to either reader.
- "A figure that cannot be computed is published as null and rendered here as not published, never as zero." Not a universal service guarantee. `computeDataRoomFeed()` uses `Promise.all` without per-block failure conversion; failures can reject the feed. `base-rates.ts` can omit metrics, return empty arrays or null weekly samples. `Figure` handles null, but many `n()` call sites assume numbers. Do not promise graceful nulls for arbitrary computation failures.
- "Every figure on this page is computed at read time from the live tables, except the change log, which is generated from git at deploy time and dated." Database provenance is correct; literal per-request recomputation is not. The feed, stats and marked-profit results have independent caches and queries do not run under a common snapshot transaction. Histories are stored observations. Publish timestamps and acknowledge caching.

## Blocks removed and remaining limits

- `funnel`: remove because its conversion percentages imply a cohort and identity nesting the service never establishes. Loads are not visitors, auth accounts are not all participants, and paid record claims are not all links.
- `traction`: remove because its lifetime totals duplicate pulse/contracts while its account curve lacks the opening balance and its composition cannot establish external demand. This also avoids placing platform account stock above the more useful daily activity.
- `shipping`: remove because commit volume and a long subject list do not answer adoption, forecast drivers or financial viability. Recent decisions and deliveries are more relevant context. Keep the omitted evidence reachable through `/api/data-room`.
- All other blocks remain. No market price or market-price history is newly printed. Profit marks remain evidence about participants, not a restatement of a floor's forecast.
- Renderer text still contains claims content alone cannot repair: "Humans only", "People trading", "Declined, with a written reason", a lead promising every figure is live, and dash glyphs for missing values. These are explicitly outside the permitted files.
- The original page has 16 headings, despite the brief's approximate count of 15. The replacement has 8. Its directives cannot split `window` into metric-specific sections or remove individual charts within a retained block. The proposal separates those follow-up changes from the completed prose edit.
